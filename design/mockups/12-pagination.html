How pages work design note

A document is cut into pages, not left as one endless scroll

117 sentences in one list means scrolling for a minute to reach the middle, losing your place whenever anything redraws, and no way to say where you are. Cut into pages, every one of those problems goes away at once.

Where the cuts fall

A page holds whole paragraphs, never half of one, and fills up at roughly nine sentences. So pages vary a little in length, the way printed ones do — and a paragraph and its sentences are never split across a turn, which would make the paragraph translation impossible to judge.

What the numbers are attached to

A page is a range of paragraphs, so page 3 means the same paragraphs on a phone and on a desktop, before and after a font-size change. The bookmark records the page and the segment — never a pixel offset. That is what stops the jump to the end of the document after an unrelated redraw.

What happens when the boundaries move

Joining or breaking a sentence changes how many sentences a paragraph holds, so a page can gain or lose one. The paragraphs on a page do not change — only how full it is. Your bookmark follows the paragraph, not the number.

What filtering does to pages

Filtering to "not settled" re-cuts the document over what is left, and says so: page 2 of 4, showing 31 of 117. Turning the filter off puts you back on the paragraph you were looking at, not on page 1.

Reading straight through

Turning past the last page moves to the next one; there is no wrap and no infinite loop. The last page says it is the last.
